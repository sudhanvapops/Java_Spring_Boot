### So Hibernate may choose to:

1. Fetch all matching joined rows from the database.

2. Reconstruct the complete Department objects and their employee collections.

3. Apply pagination in memory, selecting the first two departments.

3. Why is this bad?
Imagine:
- 100,000 departments
- 10 employees per department
- You request 20 departments

Instead of fetching approximately 20 departments and their employees, 
Hibernate might retrieve up to 1,000,000 joined rows 
and then select 20 departments in application memory.


That wastes database resources, network bandwidth, and application memory.


### Solution

2 Query Approch

1: Fetch Paginated Department ID's
2: Fetch those departments with thier Employess

So instead of getting 2 It Dpartment
We get 2 Distincts Departments
with thier employees full that might be 5,8, anything

### The key difference:
- Query 1 uses pagination to select the two departments.
- Query 2 fetches all employees belonging to those departments.
- Hibernate assembles the SQL rows into two Department objects with complete employee lists.


### Remember this for interviews

- Fetch join: Loads related entities together to avoid N+1 queries.
- Pagination: Retrieves a limited subset of records.
- The problem: Collection fetch joins multiply SQL rows, making database-level pagination unreliable for complete parent entities.
- The solution: Paginate parent IDs first, then fetch their associations in a second query.
